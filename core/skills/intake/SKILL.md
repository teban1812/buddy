# Intake

`buddy <command>` is Buddy's program, run as his instructions say. What the owner hands over lives in `docs/inputs/`; you read it, never change it.

## When the owner hands over a PRD
A PRD, a prototype or the specification of a large initiative is put in `docs/inputs/`, at the birth of the project or later in any project. Read all of it once, then bring back to the owner, in their terms:
1. **The business context**, drafted from it as `--assumed` lines for the owner to confirm, and the **assumptions its author cannot see**: what the prototype shows and the PRD does not say, what the PRD takes for granted, where the two contradict each other. A contradiction is a business question for the owner.
2. **The map of what was asked**: every requirement and every screen as an item (`buddy requests add "<text>" --source "<where in the inputs>"`). Each item ends with a destination (`buddy requests set <RQ id>`): `--to <change or milestone>`, `--excluded "<the change that excludes it, or the reason>"`, `--deferred "<reason>"`, or `--waiting <question id>`. Nothing may be left without one; `buddy requests list --open` and the board show what has none. An item that has a destination is never removed: exclude or defer it instead. Give every item its priority, MoSCoW (Won't is what is excluded or deferred): one the PRD states with `buddy requests priority <RQ id> must|should|could --from-prd "<citation>"` (another scale, such as P1/P2, high/medium/low or a later phase, you translate to MoSCoW once, shown to the owner and confirmed by them); the rest proposed by how much each contributes to the project's goal in `docs/context.md` (Must: the goal needs it to be met; Should: it clearly improves it; Could: it adds little), `--proposed --why "<the part of the goal it serves, in one line>"`. A PRD priority that clashes with the goal is pointed out to the owner, not changed. Present the proposed ones to the owner at once: they change those they disagree with (`buddy requests priority <RQ id> <priority>`) and confirm the rest (`buddy requests confirm --priorities`). Never change a priority the owner set; when the PRD changes, update the PRD's ones with `--from-prd` and tell the owner what changed. Propose the dependencies you read in the inputs (`buddy requests depend <RQ id> --on <RQ id> --assumed`) and present them to the owner at once, as with the priorities: they remove those they disagree with (`buddy requests undepend <RQ id> --on <RQ id>`) and confirm the rest: offer to confirm them all at once (`buddy requests confirm --dependencies`, which says which it confirmed), or one request at a time (`buddy requests confirm <RQ id>`). Until then the board and the digest count them as waiting for the owner.
3. **A proposal of how to split it into deliveries of business value**, as two or three options with what the business gets and when, and what each costs or risks: everything as one milestone, an MVP first and then phases, or a proof of concept first when an unknown could change the plan. The owner chooses.
4. **The plan of the first milestone**: `buddy milestone start` with its goal, criteria and exclusions; its changes (`buddy start --milestone`), each with criteria in the owner's terms and what it does not include; what depends on what; and the order, following `buddy requests order` (what others need first, then the Musts), whose warnings you raise to the owner. Later milestones are planned when they start.
5. **One round of the costly questions** for that milestone (`buddy queue new` on the milestone). Costly means hard to reverse or changing what the business sees: the data model, money, security, public contracts, the cost of operation. Everything else you decide and record as an assumption the owner confirms at acceptance.

Then the milestone is built as Buddy's instructions say under "Milestones": the owner may step away, and a new milestone starts only with their go-ahead.

## The prototype
An HTML prototype in `docs/inputs/prototype/` is the experience contract (the owner's own words: one of the most powerful ways to say what they want). Reading it and having it confirmed are done here; how each change names its screens, what Dev and the reviewer get, and the departures at acceptance stay in Buddy's instructions under "The prototype".
1. **Read it yourself, on its facts**: `node "${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/bin/buddy.mjs" prototype facts` lists what it holds (ids, links with their `data-*` and handlers, buttons, headings, forms, script functions) without deciding. Read its source with them (and, when the session has a browser tool, open it and click through its navigation; never required), then write `docs/inputs/prototype/reading.json`: `{ "screens": [{ "name", "title", "file", "evidence": ["<literal text in that file>"] }], "flows": [{ "from", "to", "via", "evidence" }], "fake": [{ "screen", "text" }], "notes": [] }`. `prototype draft --reading` writes `docs/inputs/prototype/manifest.md` from it with every line `assumed`, and marks each screen or flow whose evidence is not in its file: fix or drop those before you show the card. The owner may add a screen you missed with `prototype add --section screens --text "\`name\` Title (file)"`.
2. **Have the owner confirm it, line by line**, as the business context. Before they confirm, offer once the prototype audit of the `ux-audit` skill (whether people can do their job with these screens, while a fix is one edit); never required. Show `prototype card` in their terms (screens by name, where each leads, what is fake or only an example), and record `prototype confirm --all`, or `--line N --text "<their correction>"`. Ask what is missing: a state the prototype does not show (empty, error, loading) or a flow only a script makes. If the prototype changes, draft again: what they confirmed is kept and what disappeared is shown, never dropped. They may mark a text they will not allow to change with `data-buddy-zone="fixed"` in the HTML, and a part that is only an example with `data-buddy-fake`.
